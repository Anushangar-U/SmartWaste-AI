# Project triage policy

This is a university decision-support policy, not a legal deadline or medical protocol.
The model proposes collection priority. Authorized staff approve or override before assignment.
Review urgency is separate: high-severity analysis, affirmative hazard context or critical
priority requires urgent review, even when proposed collection priority is low. Uncertain
hazards or missing decision-relevant facts require elevated review. Queue ordering uses
review urgency first; staff retain the final operational decision.

Explicit English denials such as 'not hazardous' and 'no syringes' do not alone establish
an affirmative hazard. Simple clause/negation rules are conservative and limited; 'not sure',
'cannot rule out', possible hazards and contradictory affirmative clauses require review.
They are not a general language-understanding guarantee. Generated recommendation prose is
excluded from keyword risk scanning. Genuine hazards remain review-required.

Optional duration, location type and hazard observations are requested before submission.
Submission is always allowed without answers. Missing extracted facts produce up to three
predefined follow-up questions for staff; they do not trigger invented answers. Citizens
must not approach suspected hazards to answer. No protected or financial details are requested.
Human reviewers should evaluate the original text, extracted facts and actual cited passages.
